What twenty days in hot salt water do to carbon, basalt, and their hybrids

Four eight-ply laminates were laid up by hand, aged in two purpose-built IoT chambers, and broken in three-point bending and short-beam shear. This page rebuilds the study so you can turn the laminates over, watch the tests, and read every specimen.

Four laminates, one question

Carbon is stiff and strong but corrodes the aluminium it touches. Basalt is inert and cheap but half as stiff. The hybrids put basalt on the faces, where the metal is, and carbon in the core, where the stiffness comes from. Click a laminate to explode its stack above.

    Why aging is slow, and why it matters

    Water enters epoxy by diffusion. For a laminate a few millimetres thick at typical diffusivities, ten or twenty days is far from saturation, so the aged coupons were wet at the surface and still dry in the core. The model below is Fick's law for a plate wetted from both faces. It is a model, not something the project measured.
